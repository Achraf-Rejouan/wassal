## Re-basing the scale ladder

The standard ladder walks 100 → 1 000 → 10 000 → 100 000 *users*. That axis is meaningless
here: Wassal has no users, and its load is generated by a simulator pinned at 300 couriers
(F-13). Walking a user ladder would produce four tiers of "yes, comfortably" and teach
nothing — the failure mode the ladder exists to prevent.

The ladder is therefore re-based on **active couriers**, which is the dimension that
actually drives every resource in the design: geo-index size, location ingest rate,
candidate-search cost, WebSocket connections and claim contention all scale from it. Order
rate is held at the briefed ratio of 1 order per 6 couriers per minute.

The purpose is unchanged: confirm the path from here to there is a sequence of changes
rather than a rewrite.

## Scale Ladder

### 300 couriers — the design target

**Holds?** Yes.
**First constraint:** none reached. Every subsystem sits one to two orders of magnitude
below its ceiling. Location ingest is 100 msg/s against a Redis ceiling around 5 000/s;
`GEOSEARCH` over N=300 is sub-millisecond against a 50 ms budget; 50 orders/min is ~0.8
claims/s against a contention ceiling near 2 000/s.
**Required change:** none.
**Rough monthly cost:** €0 local. Hosted, see Phase 10.

### 3 000 couriers

**Holds?** Yes, unchanged.
**First constraint:** **location ingest at 1 000 msg/s**, hitting the tracking-service HTTP
layer and Redis `GEOADD` throughput. Redis handles it; the JVM's request-handling overhead
per message becomes the visible cost, at roughly 1 000 HTTP requests/s per tracking
instance.
**Required change:** none strictly. Batching position reports client-side (one request
carrying N positions) would cut request overhead ~10× and is a ~4 hour change. Cold-path
flush batches grow but stay well inside Postgres capability.
**Rough monthly cost:** unchanged — same containers.

### 30 000 couriers

**Holds?** With changes.
**First constraint:** **the Redis geo index becomes a single-threaded hot spot.** At 10 000
msg/s of `GEOADD` plus ~80 candidate searches/s, Redis is doing ~10 000 ops/s on one core —
reachable but no longer comfortable, and `GEOSEARCH` over N=30 000 moves from microseconds
to low milliseconds. Postgres cold-path writes reach ~1 000 rows/s batched, which is fine,
but `location_history` reaches ~860 M rows/day, making the 7-day retention (A-05) a ~6 B row
table — partition management becomes mandatory rather than merely tidy.
**Required change:** shard the geo index by geohash prefix across Redis instances (~2 days),
and move `location_history` to daily partitions with automated drop (already designed,
becomes load-bearing). Tracking-service scales horizontally, which it already supports.
**Rough monthly cost:** ~3× — a second Redis and a larger Postgres.

### 300 000 couriers

**Holds?** No, not in this shape — and the honest answer is that the *domain* changes before
the architecture does.
**First constraint:** **the single Postgres instance holding both authoritative claim state
and 8.6 B rows/day of history.** Claim throughput would need ~8 000 claims/s against a
row-lock-contention ceiling around 2 000/s.
**Required change:** genuinely structural. Split `location_history` to a time-series store
(ClickHouse or Timescale), shard the claim path by city or region — which is the real
insight, since 300 000 couriers is not one city and geographic partitioning arrives
naturally rather than as a scaling hack. Multi-region is an explicit non-goal (F-10), so
this tier is out of scope by design rather than by oversight.
**Rough monthly cost:** two orders of magnitude above the €10 ceiling. Not a target.

### Summary

| Tier | Holds | First constraint | Change required | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | **Yes** | None reached | None | — |
| 3 000 | Yes | Per-message HTTP overhead at 1 000 msg/s | Optional client-side batching | ~4 h |
| 30 000 | With changes | Single-threaded Redis geo index | Shard by geohash; enforce partition drop | ~2 d |
| 300 000 | **No** | Single Postgres for claim + history | Regional sharding, separate time-series store | Weeks — out of scope |

**The architecture holds for a 100× increase over its target before requiring structural
change**, and the changes at 30 000 are additive (shard, partition) rather than a rewrite.
That is the property the ladder exists to confirm. It is worth noting that this headroom is
mostly *incidental* — it comes from the target being small, not from the design being
especially scalable. The design's real merit is in correctness under contention, not
throughput.

## Bottleneck Inventory

| # | Component | Limit | Triggered by | Detection | Remedy | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-1 | **Expiry sweeper batch** | `LIMIT 100` per 250 ms tick = **400 expiries/s** | Stress profile: many concurrent offers expiring together. At 1 000 simultaneous expiries, the sweep takes 2.5 s and **breaches NFR-004's ±1 s** | `sweeper_lag_seconds` gauge — but only if the alert threshold is set below 1 s | Raise batch to 500; make it adaptive on observed lag | **Reachable at the target under stress** — see F-4 |
| B-2 | Row-lock contention on `couriers` | ~2 000 claims/s | Stress profile converging orders on few couriers | Claim retry counter; Postgres `pg_locks` wait time | Optimistic retry with jitter. Not fixable by sharding | 300 000 |
| B-3 | Redis single-threaded command loop | ~5 000 ops/s comfortable | Location ingest ≥ 5 000 msg/s | `redis_commands_processed_total` rate; latency histogram | Shard by geohash prefix | 30 000 |
| B-4 | Outbox publisher poll batch | 100 rows / 100 ms = **1 000 events/s per service** | Order burst above ~1 000/s | Outbox depth gauge — the earliest and most reliable signal in the system | Larger batch; shorter interval; then CDC (ADR-0006) | 30 000 |
| B-5 | Postgres connection pool | Default HikariCP 10 per service × 6 services = 60 | Concurrent claim load plus sweeper plus publisher **in the same service** | Pool wait-time metric | Size pools explicitly per service; separate pool for the sweeper | **Reachable now** — see F-5 |
| B-6 | Gateway heap per WebSocket session | ~10 k sessions/instance | 500 target is 5% of ceiling | Heap gauge; session count | Horizontal — already supported | 30 000 |
| B-7 | Simulator single instance | ~5 000 simulated couriers per JVM | Determinism forbids horizontal scaling (NFR-008) | Simulator tick-lag metric | Accept. Determinism is worth more than simulator scale | 30 000 |
| B-8 | Redpanda single broker | Fine to ~50 MB/s | Never at this scale | Broker metrics | Add brokers | 300 000 |

**B-1 and B-5 are the two reachable at the design target**, and both were invisible in the
architecture document because it stated mechanisms without stating their throughput. That is
the review earning its place.

## Component Risk

| Component | Scalability risk | Operational risk | Reversibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gateway` | Low | Medium — WebSocket lifecycle is the least familiar area (F-20) | **High** | Stateless; holds no domain data |
| `order-service` | Low | Low | **High** | Thin. Its boundary justification is weak — see F-1 |
| `dispatch-service` | Medium — the contention point | **High** — owns claim, sweeper and saga; the most complex service by a wide margin | **Low** | The heart of the project. Splitting it further would break the atomic claim (ADR-0002) |
| `tracking-service` | Medium | Low | High | Isolated and simple |
| `simulator` | Accepted (single instance) | Medium — largest component, produces no feature | High | The scope risk (T-1), not a runtime risk |
| **Postgres + partial unique indexes** | Medium | Low | **Very low** | **The lowest-reversibility choice in the system.** INV-1 and INV-2 are structurally enforced by these indexes; removing them downgrades the invariants to convention |
| Redis (geo + Pub/Sub + hot cache) | Medium | Medium — three distinct roles in one instance | High | Roles are separable later; see F-3 on ownership |
| Redpanda | Low | **High** — Kafka operations is a named gap (F-20) | Medium | Topic and consumer-group design is hard to change once events are in flight |
| Outbox + inbox pattern | Low | Low | **Low** | Every service depends on it; the schema shape is now also constrained by CDC compatibility (ADR-0006) |
| Saga orchestration | Low | Medium | **Low** | Moving to choreography would lose resumability, which was the deciding property |

**Three low-reversibility choices**, and they are worth a second look now while looking is
cheap: the partial unique indexes, the outbox/inbox pattern, and saga orchestration. All
three survive scrutiny — each is load-bearing for a named invariant, and each was chosen
against a specific alternative rather than by default. The indexes in particular are *worth*
their irreversibility: they convert INV-1 from "enforced by tested code" to "cannot be
violated".

## Unnecessary Complexity

Applying ADR-0001's test to every element: **name the failure it isolates or the scaling axis
it unlocks.** Removal is a finding of equal standing to addition.

| # | Element | Justification given | Holds up? | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `tracking-service` as a separate deployable | Isolates a 100 msg/s write path from the correctness path | **Yes — and more strongly than stated.** The decisive argument was missing from the architecture: keeping tracking separate means the headline chaos experiment can kill `dispatch-service` *without also stopping location ingest*. Merging them would contaminate the recovery measurement, since NFR-005 could not distinguish "recovered assignment" from "recovered ingest" | **Keep.** Add the chaos-isolation argument to ADR-0002 |
| 2 | `order-service` as a separate deployable | Isolates order acceptance from dispatch availability | **Only partly.** That isolation is provided by the **Kafka topic**, not by the separate deployable — merging order-service into gateway would preserve it entirely. The separateness is currently justified by a failure the test plan never induces | **Keep, but earn it** — see F-1 |
| 3 | Two Kafka topics | Each carries a named async boundary | **Yes.** `order.lifecycle` is exactly what the chaos suite exploits. `assignment.lifecycle` feeds reconciliation and client notification | Keep |
| 4 | A third `courier.lifecycle` topic | Not proposed | n/a — correctly absent | Confirm it stays absent. Its only consumer would be its own producer |
| 5 | Redis serving three roles (geo index, hot cache, Pub/Sub) | Not separately justified | **Yes**, but ownership was muddled — see F-3 | Keep one instance; fix ownership |
| 6 | Separate `reconciliation-job` | Independence from service code (R-9) | **Yes, but insufficient as designed** — see F-2, the Blocker |
| 7 | Schema-per-service in one Postgres | Memory budget; atomic claim needs one transaction | **Yes**, and the limitation is already recorded honestly in ADR-0002 | Keep. Do not add Postgres instances |
| 8 | OTel Collector as a separate container | Trace aggregation | **Weakly.** Services could export to Prometheus directly and skip the collector, saving ~256 MB against a tight budget (F-6) | **Keep, but only because** distributed tracing across four services is a stated learning target and the collector is where sampling and context propagation are actually configured. Marginal call, recorded as such |
| 9 | Toxiproxy | Network fault injection for chaos | Yes — no substitute in the stack | Keep. `tools` profile only, never in `core` |
| 10 | Grafana | Dashboards as the evidence surface (FR-023) | Yes — three panels appear in the README | Keep, in `observability` profile only |

**Nothing is recommended for removal.** That is an unusual outcome for a complexity audit
and deserves scepticism rather than satisfaction, so it is worth stating why it is credible
here: the element list was already filtered twice — once by ADR-0001's test at design time,
and once by Phase 1's complexity-reduction pass, which cut a routing engine, four Grafana
panels, a full REST surface and five candidate Kafka topics before this document existed.
The audit's value this time was **F-2 and F-3**, which found complexity that was
*insufficient or misassigned* rather than excessive.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Recommendation | Applied to `04-architecture.md` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F-2** | **Blocker** | **The reconciliation job as designed is partly self-referential.** It compares Postgres live state against the Postgres event log — but the outbox row and the state change are written in the *same transaction* (FR-013), so they cannot disagree. Comparing them proves nothing about INV-5; it is close to a tautology, which is precisely risk R-9 | Reconciliation must read **three independent sources**: (1) Postgres live state, (2) the Kafka topics consumed independently from offset 0, (3) the simulator's ground-truth sink. State-vs-outbox is near-tautological; **state-vs-Kafka** proves the publisher worked; **both-vs-ground-truth** proves the whole system did | **Yes** |
| **F-1** | **Major** | **`order-service`'s boundary is justified by a failure nobody tests.** The claimed isolation is "orders survive a dead dispatcher", which the Kafka topic provides regardless of whether order-service is a separate process. No chaos scenario kills `order-service` | Add a chaos scenario: kill `order-service` mid-creation, assert no order is half-created and the outbox drains on recovery. Cheap (~2 h) and it converts a claimed boundary into a demonstrated one. Merging into `gateway` is the alternative and is **not** recommended — it would put domain state in the edge tier | **Yes** |
| **F-3** | **Major** | **Geo-index ownership is contradictory.** ADR-0003 says Redis holds "available couriers only", which requires `tracking-service` to know courier availability — but availability is owned by `dispatch-service`, and module boundaries forbid tracking from reading dispatch's schema | Split the two concerns in Redis: **`tracking-service` owns the geo index of *all* courier positions; `dispatch-service` owns an `available` set.** Candidate search is `GEOSEARCH` intersected with that set. Ownership becomes clean — tracking owns *where*, dispatch owns *who is free* — with no cross-schema read and no new failure mode | **Yes** |
| **F-4** | **Major** | **Sweeper throughput ceiling breaches NFR-004 under the stress profile.** `LIMIT 100` per 250 ms tick caps expiry at 400/s. The stress profile deliberately creates mass simultaneous expiry; at 1 000 concurrent expiries the sweep takes 2.5 s, exceeding the ±1 s requirement | Raise batch to 500, make it adaptive on observed lag, and set the `sweeper_lag_seconds` alert at **0.5 s** — below the requirement, so the alert fires before the SLO breaks rather than with it | **Yes** |
| **F-5** | **Major** | **Connection-pool exhaustion is reachable at the design target.** `dispatch-service` runs claim traffic, the 250 ms sweeper and the 100 ms outbox publisher against one default 10-connection HikariCP pool. Under stress, background work and claim traffic compete, and the failure presents as latency rather than error | Size pools explicitly per service, and give the sweeper and publisher their **own small dedicated pools** so background work cannot starve the claim path. Export pool wait time | **Yes** |
| **F-6** | **Major** | **NFR-011's 6 GB target is not achievable with default settings.** Six JVMs at default heap (~1 GB each on a large host) plus Redpanda's default memory reservation exceeds it before Postgres is counted. The NFR is currently aspirational | Explicit caps: `-XX:MaxRAMPercentage=50` with container limits ~384 MB per service JVM; Redpanda `--memory 1G --overprovisioned --smp 1`; Postgres `shared_buffers=256MB`. Measured total ≈ 5.2 GB with two gateways. **Compose memory limits are a requirement, not a nicety** | **Yes** |
| **F-7** | Minor | **The demo does not exercise its own headline capability.** Cross-instance WebSocket fan-out is proven only in a two-instance test. A default `docker compose up` runs one gateway, so a Reader never sees the mechanism the design is proudest of | Run **two gateway instances by default** behind a lightweight reverse proxy in the `core` profile. Costs ~430 MB (already in F-6's budget). Converts the fan-out claim into something a reader observes rather than reads about | **Yes** |
| **F-8** | Minor | **Re-offer latency path is ambiguous.** FR-007 requires the next candidate within 200 ms of a decline. The architecture does not state whether a decline round-trips through Kafka — if it did, the 100 ms publisher poll plus consume would consume most of the budget | Clarify: decline is handled **in-process** within `dispatch-service`, which owns the offer aggregate. No Kafka round trip on the re-offer path. Kafka carries the *notification* of the outcome, not the control flow | **Yes** |
| **F-9** | Observation | **A legitimate inconsistency window exists** between `AssignmentCreated` being committed by dispatch and the order reaching `ASSIGNED` in order-service. During it, an assignment exists for an order still marked `OFFERING` | No design change — this is correct eventual consistency. But **reconciliation must classify it as in-flight rather than variance**, or the job will report false positives on every live run. Recorded so F-2's three-way comparison accounts for it | **Yes** |

## Reviewer's Summary

**The architecture first needs changing at 30 000 couriers** — a 100× increase over its
target — and the changes there are additive (shard the geo index, enforce partitions) rather
than structural. The path from here to there is a sequence of changes, not a rewrite.

**Top three bottlenecks with their detection signals:**

1. **Expiry sweeper at 400/s** (B-1) — detected by `sweeper_lag_seconds`, alerting at 0.5 s.
   Reachable *at the design target* under the stress profile, which makes it the most urgent
   finding after the Blocker.
2. **Connection-pool exhaustion in `dispatch-service`** (B-5) — detected by pool wait time.
   Also reachable now, and it presents as latency rather than as an error, which is the worst
   way for a bottleneck to present.
3. **Row-lock contention on hot couriers** (B-2) — detected by claim-retry rate. Not
   fixable by sharding, and deliberately left in place because exploring it is the point of
   the project.

**Recommended for removal: nothing** — with the caveat above that this is credible only
because two prior passes already cut a routing engine, five topics and most of the REST
surface.

**The finding that mattered most** is the Blocker: the reconciliation job would have compared
Postgres against Postgres and reported a confident zero. It would have passed. It would have
been in the README. And it would have proven nothing — the exact failure mode discovery
listed as the second-worst possible outcome for this project.
